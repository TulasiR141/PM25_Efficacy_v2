# Project Poster Documentation

## Overview

This document describes the academic poster created for the **SpheroidMetrics: Automated Segmentation and Morphological Analysis of Cancer Spheroids in Bright-Field Images** project.

## Design Tool

**Canva** - A web-based graphic design platform used to create the poster layout and organize content sections.

## Poster Content Structure

### Background - Why is this research important?
- Pancreatic cancer is highly fatal and hard to treat
- 3D spheroids offer tissue-like disease models
- They support studies of tumor growth and drug response
- Morphological metrics reveal growth and treatment effects
- Accurate analysis is crucial for reliable results

### Problem - What challenge are we addressing?
- Manual spheroid measurement is slow and subjective
- Inefficient for large datasets or time-point comparisons
- Automation is needed for faster, consistent analysis
- Automation enables accurate measurement of morphological metrics such as area, perimeter, roundness, and brightness

### Objectives - What are we trying to achieve?
1. **Spheroid Segmentation**: Implement and optimize a model (YOLO/U-Net) to accurately detect spheroid boundaries
2. **Feature Extraction**: Calculate key morphological metrics and raw pixel intensity from segmented regions
3. **Feature Comparison**: Perform comparative analysis of metrics to assess growth and drug-induced morphological changes

### Methodology - How will we accomplish this?
Pipeline stages:
- Research and Planning
- Literature Review
- Data Collection
- Image Preprocessing
- Pipeline Preparation
- Model Training and Optimisation
- Metrics Evaluation
- Performance Evaluation
- Output Generation
- Metric Calculation and Validation
- Documentation and Demo

### Challenges - What obstacles do we face?
- Limited data restricts training performance
- Debris obscures the boundary, making recognition difficult
- Irregular spheroid shapes and merging over time complicate accurate feature measurement
- Differences in lighting, focus, or image acquisition between pre-treatment and post-treatment images can affect metric comparison
